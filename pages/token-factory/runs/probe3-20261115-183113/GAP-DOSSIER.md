# Gap dossier: hero for the engineering head

**What the hero says now.** The headline reads: "Frontier open models. Served with speed, scale and privacy." The sub-line reads: "Run leading open models on Sarvam's GPUs in India, with shared or dedicated capacity and zero data retention available on request." The button reads "Get Started". It leads to the Token Factory home on indus.sarvam.ai. We have not checked what the visitor sees there.

**The gap.** The buyer question answered worst is: "Does the page say the API is OpenAI-compatible". The hero does not say it. An engineering head decides by asking how hard the switch will be. The button also does not say what happens after the click, and the headline does not say what the buyer gets. The hero names no checkable standard, so a cautious buyer has nothing to verify.

**Evidence we hold.** CL-001 says the API is OpenAI-compatible and runs on Sarvam's GPUs in India. CL-006 says the buyer can change models by changing the model name. CL-005 gives the security standards, and it is Sarvam's own statement. CL-004 covers zero data retention, and CL-013 covers sizing the deployment.

**Hypothesis.** If we change the sub-line and button to state API compatibility and the next step for engineering heads at GCC and IT services firms, more of them click Get Started, because they can see the switch is low effort and know what follows.

**What we do not know.** We have no internal analytics, so the real click rate is unknown. The positioning promise is PROPOSED and not buyer-validated. We do not know what buyers see after the click.

**What would prove it wrong.** A fair side-by-side test shows no gain in clicks for the new hero. It is also wrong if clicks rise but visitors leave at the next page.
