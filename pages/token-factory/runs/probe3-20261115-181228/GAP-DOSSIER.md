# Gap dossier (PROBE)

**Gap dossier: Why the hero CTA lets this buyer walk away**

The hero says:  
**H1:** *Frontier open models. Served with speed, scale and privacy.*  
**Sub-line:** *Run leading open models on Sarvam’s GPUs in India, with shared or dedicated capacity and zero data retention available on request.*  
**Button:** *Get Started* (links to the product console on indus.sarvam.ai).

An engineering head at a GCC or services firm is a methodical technical evaluator. They want to know what happens when they click. “Get Started” is too opaque: am I walking into a sign‑up form, a sandbox, a free trial, or a scheduled sales call? The button fails the need “Tell me what I’m committing to.” Without that clarity, the buyer assumes the worst – a friction‑heavy, time‑wasting gate – and leaves.

We hold evidence that Token Factory delivers an India‑hosted, OpenAI‑compatible API (CL‑001, CL‑002), zero‑data‑retention (CL‑004), enterprise‑grade certifications (CL‑005), a single‑key catalogue (CL‑006), transparent pricing (CL‑011), and sizing guidance (CL‑013). None of that reaches the CTA; the button alone carries none of the positioning promise we have set out (PROPOSED): “Put a production model workload on an India‑served API, then prove it meets your quality, speed and cost bar.” The gap is the missing handshake between the offer and the first action.

**Hypothesis:** If we replace the “Get Started” label and surrounding microcopy with a clear, low‑risk next‑step descriptor (for example, “Try the API free – get your key in 5 minutes”), more engineering heads will click through and begin a trial, because the commitment is explicit and the fear of a sales encounter disappears.

**What we don’t know:** We have no internal analytics, so the current click‑through rate is unknown.  
**Disproof:** If a controlled test shows no lift in console visits – or a drop – the hypothesis is wrong.
