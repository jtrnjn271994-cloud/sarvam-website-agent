# Page context pack: Sarvam Token Factory

Each bullet ends with its source ids and its label. A market signal shows demand for a kind of product. It is never proof about Sarvam.

## 1. Positioning promise

- Promise, exact words: "Put a production model workload on an India-served API, then prove it meets your quality, speed and cost bar." [LIB-115, SRC-058 | PROPOSED]
- Page message to test, exact words. Headline: "Serve the model that passes your workload. From India." Support: "Test Sarvam and other makers' open-weight models on your tasks. Compare quality, response time and total operating cost before moving production traffic." [LIB-115 | PROPOSED]
- The proposed advantage is the "verified fit of a particular model, workload and operating boundary", alongside access to Sarvam's own models. It is not a lead in speed, price, quality or adoption. [LIB-115 | PROPOSED]
- CTA wording conflict: the Seven Pages say "Compare on my workload"; the buyer file says "Compare on your workload". A person must pick one. [LIB-115, LIB-148 | PROPOSED]

## 2. Category and first purchase

- Category: managed model inference, an API that runs a model for an application. Not GPU rental, not an agent platform, not a frontier-model claim. [LIB-115, LIB-118 | PROPOSED, AUDIT]
- First purchase: move one repeatable workload after a matched test against its current endpoint or internal stack. Expansion comes only when quality, operations and economics hold. [LIB-115, LIB-116 | PROPOSED]
- Commercial unit: count accepted results, not tokens. Cost per accepted result = all serving charges + integration + review + support + retries, divided by accepted results. [LIB-115 | PROPOSED]
- A token is a unit of text the model reads or writes. Define it on the page at first use. [LIB-115 | PROPOSED]

## 3. Primary audience and trigger

- Primary audience: enterprise IT services and GCC leaders who serve clients in all industries. Their teams run the test. [LIB-148, SRC-028 | PROPOSED]
- Conflict to keep visible: the Seven Pages name the AI platform lead at a GCC or IT services firm as the first buyer. The buyer file makes that person a supporting role. [LIB-115, LIB-148 | PROPOSED]
- Trigger: a model works in a trial but stays hard to serve under real traffic. The team needs a supported model, response time, a data boundary and a cost it can defend. [LIB-115, LIB-116 | PROPOSED]
- Workload in our data: "Moving an OpenAI-compatible workload to a new inference provider". Ambition: become the token provider of their choice. [LIB-148 | PROPOSED]
- GTM status conflict: the GCC inference row says "prove"; the Token Factory card says "later". Both are research priorities. [LIB-055, LIB-058 | PROPOSED]

## 4. Supporting audiences and what each must see

- Technical evaluator, who runs the test (BR-001): how to test one workload, the OpenAI-compatible API, where inference runs, a price per 1M tokens, and who owns fine-tuned weights. [BR-001, EV-008, EV-009, EV-015 | PROPOSED]
- IT services champion, the practice head (BR-005): switch models by changing the model name, a way to test a client workload, and no "sovereign" label. Objection: the firm already holds frontier-model deals. [BR-005, EV-013 | PROPOSED]
- Risk and compliance (BR-003): where inference runs, zero retention on request, certifications, and operational logs under separate terms. [BR-003, EV-009, EV-011, EV-012 | PROPOSED]
- Procurement (BR-004): a price unit with the tax rule (excluding GST), SLA terms set by contract, and how to request security documents. [BR-004, EV-010, EV-012 | PROPOSED]
- GCC head, India centre budget (BR-002): proof from a real deployment, a price unit, one low-effort next step, and no savings claim without a matched test. [BR-002, EV-015 | PROPOSED]
- IT services economic buyer (BR-006): the model list, prices and India serving. Objection: the margin is too thin after model costs. [BR-006, EV-014 | PROPOSED]
- Other-industry builders: the GTM data marks banks, insurance, healthcare and telecom "later" for inference. [LIB-055 | PROPOSED]

## 5. The real alternatives and what beats each

- Keep an internal serving stack (control over models and tuning). What beats it: compare operating hours, GPU use, reliability and total cost on one workload. [LIB-115 | PROPOSED]
- Global managed providers: Nebius, Fireworks, Together (model choice, production controls, visible customer proof). What beats it: the same model and version, same traffic, matched test. [LIB-115, EV-018, EV-020, EV-021 | PROPOSED, VENDOR]
- India providers: E2E Blaze, Yotta Shakti Studio, Krutrim (India infrastructure, local buying). What beats it: compare the exact model, terms and support. E2E also lists Sarvam's models, so it is a channel too. [LIB-115, LIB-001, EV-019, EV-022, EV-023 | PROPOSED, VENDOR]
- An existing cloud platform, such as Amazon Bedrock (contracts, identity, tools). What beats it: a workload gain large enough to justify change; Sarvam may sit beside it. [LIB-115, LIB-118 | PROPOSED, AUDIT]
- An aggregator, read as OpenRouter: the Tech Mahindra CEO says "open router". OpenRouter's Standard plan fee is 5.5% on credit purchases. [EV-001, LIB-084 | CHECKED, PROPOSED]
- The IT firm's own platform, such as Coforge AI Launchpad, which offers "the right models for different workloads". Our reading: it can be a rival or a route. [EV-006 | CHECKED, PROPOSED]
- Rivals win enterprise inference through cloud marketplaces and commit spend (Fireworks via Azure, AWS, Google). Sarvam's listing there is not checked. [LIB-005 | UNCHECKED]

## 6. Rival gaps from the UI/UX audit

- No customer names or logos on the Sarvam page. Fireworks, Together and Yotta Shakti Cloud show a customer section. [SRC-083, SRC-074 | PROPOSED, VENDOR]
- No deployment-options section. Fireworks and both Together pages have one. [SRC-083, SRC-077, SRC-078 | PROPOSED, VENDOR]
- No top-bar Pricing link. Pricing first appears about halfway down ("Compare pricing"). [SRC-083, SRC-074 | PROPOSED, VENDOR]
- No comparison table. E2E Blaze has two: what a team stops operating, and models. [SRC-083, SRC-076 | PROPOSED, VENDOR]
- Hero buttons: Sarvam styles "Explore models" as primary and "Get Started" second. No hero button offers a workload test. [SRC-083, SRC-074 | PROPOSED, VENDOR]
- Strength to keep: a security section with ISO 27001:2022 and SOC 2 Type II; few rivals show one. [SRC-083, EV-012 | PROPOSED, VENDOR]
- The proposed reading: Sarvam may be the only page here that shows India processing, an uptime SLA and zero retention together. A missing rival feature does not prove the rival lacks it. [LIB-117 | PROPOSED]

## 7. What the position does NOT claim

- India serving, INR billing and an OpenAI-compatible API are not exclusive. E2E Blaze states all three. [LIB-115, EV-019 | PROPOSED, VENDOR]
- No lower-cost claim. A cheap token that fails the task raises the real cost. [LIB-115, LIB-117 | PROPOSED]
- No speed claim. The page speed chart shows model names only, no numbers. [LIB-117, SRC-001 | PROPOSED]
- No ownership of other makers' models. Sarvam hosts the weights; it did not build them. [LIB-128, rules/brand-rules.md | VENDOR]
- Zero retention is "on request", under an agreed arrangement. It is separate from India processing. [CL-004, EV-011 | VENDOR]
- No "sovereign" label, no "best", no "only". [LIB-114, EV-005 | PROPOSED]
- No self-hosted Token Factory. The docs we hold show self-hosting for speech and Vision models only. [LIB-001, LIB-148 | VENDOR]
- Routing and in-organisation hosting stay unproven for Token Factory. [LIB-148 | PROPOSED]
