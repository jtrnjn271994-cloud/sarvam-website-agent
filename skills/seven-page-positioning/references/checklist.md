# Checklist: answer yes or no before you reply

These are the seven questions asked of the draft, then the code checks.

1. Is there exactly one first buyer, copied from the brief? (If the brief names two, did you list both in `self_check.gaps`?)
2. Does the category use the buyer's word?
3. Do the alternatives come from buyer words and the insight, not only from our rival list?
4. Is the main claim one sentence that a reader can prove true or false?
5. Does it pass the swap test for each rival row, one row at a time, with a reason for each?
6. Does every proof line have an evidence id that exists, and a label?
7. Is any proof an AUDIT note or UNCHECKED? If yes, remove it or mark it an assumption.
8. Does the claim avoid lower cost, speed, "best", "only" and "sovereign"?
9. Does it avoid every phrase on the do-not-claim list?
10. Are exactly three weak points named, and does one say where a rival is better?
11. Do the three bets differ in structure (different section after the hero, different order), not only in headline?
12. Does each bet name its `section_plan` and `baseline_change`?
13. Is the step after the main button stated as unknown when the files do not say it?
14. Are sentences about 20 words or fewer, in the active voice?
15. Does `self_check` hold every field, and does `gaps` name what the files lack?
16. If no claim backs a scale the promise names (for example speed), drop that section and list the scale under gaps. Never write a number or word that implies it. This rule applies only to new lines you add. Live baseline text is not subject to it: it stays word for word, even where no claim backs it.
